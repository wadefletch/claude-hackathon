## Who She Is

Danielle just landed a CNA position at a hospital on Chicago's South Side, paying $19.50/hour. She's never lived in Chicago. She has three weeks to find housing, move, enroll her son in first grade, and start a new job — with no car and no local network to lean on.

She's not new to hardship or new to problem-solving. Her last two cars got repossessed, so she's done budgeting her life around vehicle ownership — but that means every housing decision is now inseparable from a transit decision. She's resourceful by necessity: she's currently doing her own version of this platform by hand, with a Zillow tab open next to a CTA map tab, manually eyeballing whether a listing's commute is survivable on a 6am shift.

She approaches listings with skepticism, not naivety. She's been burned by scam listings and "too good to be true" rents before. She doesn't need to be sold to — she needs the manual cross-referencing work taken off her plate before she signs something she can't actually live with.

## What She's Looking For

Danielle is solving one interconnected decision, not six separate ones. A unit that's cheap but 50 minutes from work by bus isn't affordable — it's a childcare pickup crisis waiting to happen. A unit near work but with no grocery access within a reasonable walk/transit ride means a weekly grocery run becomes its own logistics problem on top of everything else. Her own notebook lays out her filters exactly:

1. **2 bedrooms** — enough space for her and her son, non-negotiable.
2. **Under $1,400/month** — sustainable against a ~$40K income once CTA fare and food costs are factored in, not just "affordable" by market standards.
3. **Under 40 minutes to work** — her real ceiling for a commute she has to make on a fixed 6am shift, not a soft preference.
4. **Bus or train only** — she has no car and isn't getting one; routing has to be real transit routing, not driving-based estimates.
5. **Safe area** — she's moving in alone with a 6-year-old to a city she doesn't know; safety signal matters as much as price.
6. **Near school** — her son starts 1st grade this fall. She needs to know school quality and enrollment boundary *before* she commits to a neighborhood, not after.

**Added consideration: grocery access.** Danielle also needs a walkable or short-transit trip to a real grocery store, not a food desert with only convenience stores or dollar stores. As a single parent working full shifts, she doesn't have slack time for a grocery run that eats an hour of transit each way — this should be evaluated with the same rigor as her commute-to-work filter, not treated as a lesser, secondary amenity.

She is not browsing. She is solving an urgent, multi-constraint problem on a three-week clock, alone, at night, after her son is asleep.

## How She'll Use the Platform

- **Entry point:** Likely arrives via a Facebook relocation/community group, a hospital HR onboarding referral, or a "moving to Chicago" search — not a generic real-estate ad.
- **First action:** Inputs her income, her son's age (to signal school-age household), her work address, and her must-haves (2BR, transit-only commute). She wants the platform to immediately narrow a huge, overwhelming city down to a short, defensible list — this is the core value, replacing hours of manual cross-referencing.
- **Core filtering behavior:** Sorts/filters listings by real commute time via public transit to her specific work address, rent-to-income ratio against her actual income (not a generic affordability label), nearby school quality/boundary for a 1st grader, and proximity to a full grocery store.
- **Trust behavior:** Wants verification, not marketing copy — she will not trust a listing that just says "close to transit" or "great schools nearby" without being able to see the actual commute time and actual school assignment for that address.
- **Decision speed:** Because her timeline is three weeks, she needs to go from "overwhelmed by an unfamiliar city" to "shortlist of 3–5 realistic options" in a single session, not over weeks of browsing.
- **Post-move relationship to the platform:** If she has a good experience, she becomes a referral source into the Facebook groups and community networks she trusts most — word-of-mouth from someone who successfully navigated the exact situation is more persuasive to this segment than any ad.

## Design Implications

- Commute time must be calculated by actual transit routing to a real address, not a generic transit score.
- School data (assignment boundary + quality signal) needs to be tied to the specific unit's address, not the neighborhood in general.
- Grocery access should be shown as a transit/walk time to a real full-service grocery store, same treatment as commute — not a map pin with no context, and not satisfied by a corner/convenience store.
- School quality and enrollment boundary should be surfaced as a filterable, address-level data point, not something she has to research separately after shortlisting.
- Affordability should be shown as rent-to-income against *her* stated income, not a generic "budget-friendly" tag.
- The whole experience needs to work in one urgent session — she does not have weeks to explore.
